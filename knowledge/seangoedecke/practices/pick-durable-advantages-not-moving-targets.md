# Pick advantages that resist model improvement, not ones that are a moving target

The bar for staying valuable next to a coding agent isn't "can I still write
code well" — it's *value over replacement*: how much better your output is
than a replacement-level agent doing your job, not how much revenue your work
happened to touch. Writing code now costs about a hundred dollars a month, so
"I can clear the JIRA tickets that come across my desk" stopped being worth a
salary; the live question for any engineer is what they do that the current
frontier model wouldn't do in their position, and why that's worth two or
three orders of magnitude more.

Retreating into some "harder" corner of engineering doesn't answer that
question, because model capability is a moving target, not a fixed ceiling.
"Make working changes to large codebases" was still a real human edge at the
start of 2026 and isn't anymore; if models can push the Riemann hypothesis
forward, kernel drivers and GPU shaders aren't a permanent refuge either.
Betting your value on being deeper into "hard engineering" than the model
just delays the reckoning by one model generation.

The durable move is to look for tasks models *haven't* gotten better at over
time, and that are hard for them to improve at *in principle* — not tasks you
personally find hard right now, which is a different axis entirely. Deep,
tenured familiarity with a specific codebase and durable technical
communication skill are two concrete examples that hold up under this test
(each earns its own synthesis: see the sibling ignorance/paranoia and
technical-communication concepts in this same area).

The failure mode to avoid is being a "meat proxy": someone who copies
requests into an agent and submits its output as their own work, adding no
value by definition. That's true even inside a "cunning system of multiple
agents" — a so-called software factory — because whatever technique makes
that system work eventually gets absorbed into enterprise AI tooling, at
which point the person running it becomes disposable. Simply refusing to use
AI at all is a safer fallback than being a meat proxy (you'll still do some
things better than the model would have), but the actual winning move is
finding and occupying a gap the model structurally can't close.
