# Workslop is an asymmetric-effort attack — respond by restoring the balance

Workslop — a colleague or manager communicating by pasting big chunks of
AI-generated text — is not just annoying content, it's a denial-of-service
attack disguised as communication: producing it costs the sender almost
nothing, but reading and evaluating it still costs the reader the same effort
it always did. Every defense against it is really a way to force the asymmetry
back into balance, whether by refusing the trade, closing the effort gap
yourself, or changing the channel entirely. (Not all AI-assisted messages
qualify — if the sender genuinely put real time into the content, the
asymmetry isn't there, and it deserves to be read like any human message.)

**Match the tactic to the leverage you actually have.**

- **Direct refusal, if you have the standing for it.** If you outrank the
  sender or have enough social capital — a senior engineer with an intern, say
  — the simplest fix is to just say "don't do that." It doesn't scale to your
  whole management chain, but it's the cheapest option wherever it's available.
- **Fight AI with AI.** Where you can't say no — workslop from a manager, for
  instance — paste it into your own LLM and ask for the salient points, or in
  the more aggressive version, have the LLM draft your entire reply. This is
  uncomfortable because it makes you complicit in the same low-effort exchange,
  but ten seconds of their effort shouldn't buy ten minutes of yours; matching
  effort with effort is more sustainable than eating the asymmetry every time.
- **Force a symmetric channel.** Redirect to a call or an in-person chat. This
  is really the general fix for "this person communicates badly," but it works
  especially well on AI content: workslop can't be pasted into a live
  conversation, and requiring someone to spend real synchronous time with you
  is also a good filter for people who aren't serious.
- **Ignore it.** Long status updates, external pull requests, and other
  low-stakes workslop can simply be skimmed, deprioritized, or left unanswered
  — match their lack of effort with your own rather than over-investing in
  reading something they under-invested in writing. If it's actually
  important, the sender will come back and say so in their own words.

A related, more specific tactic — treating a colleague who is just relaying
your messages into a coding agent as a high-latency interface to that agent,
rather than as a bad engineer — is covered in
[agents-raise-the-floor-not-the-ceiling](./agents-raise-the-floor-not-the-ceiling.md);
it's the same asymmetric-effort logic, applied deliberately to a colleague
using AI to do the underlying work rather than just to communicate about it.
