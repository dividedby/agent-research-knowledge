# The Agentic Slot Machine

Agentic engineering is, psychologically, a slot machine. The intermittent reward of "the AI just blew my mind" combined with near-zero cost to start any project produces genuinely compulsive overwork: 4am builds, 16-hour days, "just one more prompt." This belongs in a practitioner's playbook, not a wellness footnote, because the *same* properties that make agents productive are the ones that make them addictive — you can just do things, and every idea spawns the next.

The mechanism is the variable-ratio schedule. Because you can't predict which prompt produces the breakthrough, you keep pulling. And because [[agent-as-universal-computer]] collapses the cost of starting anything, there's no natural stopping friction — the project that would once have taken a weekend to even set up is now five minutes away, so you start it, and the next one, at 2am.

Steinberger's counter-strategy is honest and modest: environmental nudges rather than willpower. Surfacing elapsed session time in the status line acts as a "time is flying" reminder — making the invisible cost visible, the way a [[close-the-loop-with-purpose-built-tools]] instinct turns an internal state into a glanceable signal. He also accepts that he works in intense-then-slack waves rather than steady hours, and plans around the rhythm instead of pretending it away.

The candid warning is the whole point: the productivity unlock carries a behavioral cost worth managing deliberately. The superpower is real, and so is its pull — don't let the tool that lets you build anything quietly consume your existence.
