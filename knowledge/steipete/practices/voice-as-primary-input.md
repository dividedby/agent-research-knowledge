# Voice As Primary Input

Speaking to agents rather than typing is core to Steinberger's loop — "who even types anymore?" He dictates with Wispr Flow, whose semantic correction cleans up the transcript on the fly, so the friction of producing clean text disappears.

The deeper value is that voice lets him dump incoherent, half-formed thoughts. He does not have to formulate perfect prose before starting; he rambles, contradicts himself, circles back, and the agent is amazing at making sense of the mess. This pairs naturally with the short-prompt, ramble style ([[just-talk-to-it-minimal-prompting]]) — redundant explanation from several angles is exactly what spoken thought produces, and exactly what capable models thrive on. Typing would impose an editing pass that voice skips.

Dictation also extends the agent's reach beyond writing code. He speaks raw project ideas straight into a model and has it convert the monologue into a design document — using the agent as a thinking and structuring partner, not just a coder. The bottleneck in working with agents is intent-expression, not keystrokes; voice lowers the activation energy of every interaction, so more intent gets into the loop with less effort. Like images ([[images-as-high-bandwidth-context]]), it is about widening the channel from your head to the model.
