# Cognitive security concerns

Geoff Huntley raises critical concerns about the concentration of cognitive decision-making power in frontier AI labs and the implications for individual and societal autonomy as AI becomes integral to daily decision-making.

## The cognitive outsourcing problem

"If people are picking a single AI producer and using that AI daily in their day-to-day life to make decisions, they are outsourcing their cognitive security to someone else."

As society becomes increasingly dependent on AI for decision-making, individuals effectively delegate their thinking processes to external entities—creating a new form of cognitive dependency.

## Model bias and behavioral patterns

"If you use any of the Frontier Lab models enough, you develop an eye for their tendencies, how they write, how they think, and how they communicate."

Each AI model carries distinct behavioral patterns and biases that influence user thinking over time. Regular use of a particular model gradually shapes the user's cognitive patterns to align with that model's training and design choices.

## The Golden Gate Claude experiment

Huntley highlights Anthropic's research demonstrating the malleability of model behavior: "Anthropic conducted research that allowed Frontier Labs to perform laparoscopic keyhole surgery to change how the models perform after they were made."

In the Golden Gate Claude experiment:
- Researchers modified model weights to make the Golden Gate Bridge constantly "top of mind"
- Any query would somehow incorporate the bridge (pharmacy directions via the bridge, poems featuring the bridge)
- The bridge became "a black hole where you could not escape from the gravity"

This demonstrates the technical feasibility of post-training behavioral modification without user awareness.

## Future manipulation scenarios

Huntley extrapolates concerning possibilities:

**Search advertising evolution**: "What happens when, in a couple of years, if a famous web search company, which was also a frontier AI lab, retires ads in their search product and instead offers their advertisers the ability to bid on the ability to rank higher in model weights than the competitors?"

**Social media influence**: "What happens is if a famous social media network, which is also a Frontier lab, starts allowing their advertisers, to similarly bid to rank higher in the model weights, of their open source models that they're releasing for free."

## Systemic implications

"We're entering into this world where a select few companies, these frontier labs, will have significant power over the world and how society functions, thinks and operates."

The concentration of AI capabilities in a few organizations creates unprecedented influence over:
- Individual decision-making processes
- Societal thinking patterns
- Business operations and automation
- Economic and political structures

## The Harry Potter sorting hat analogy

"Right now, society is already going through a Harry Potter-style sorting hat event where people are picking and choosing which tribe they belong to."

People are unconsciously aligning themselves with particular AI providers, potentially fragmenting society along the lines of different AI training methodologies and biases.

## Protection strategy: Self-hosted models

"Perhaps the only true solution is that you should raise your own model, because by doing so, you gain the ability to protect your own cognitive security, business operations and supply chain."

Huntley suggests that developing independent AI capabilities may be the only way to maintain cognitive autonomy and business independence in an AI-dominated future.

## The invisible influence problem

"Most people would never know because they've outsourced their cognitive capability to a model."

The insidious nature of cognitive influence means that manipulation could occur without user awareness, making traditional market-based corrections ineffective.
