# AI as economic warfare

Geoff Huntley theorizes that the release of high-quality open-source AI models by non-U.S. entities represents a form of economic warfare, weaponizing the traditional open-source strategy of destroying commercial markets through free alternatives.

## Historical precedent: Open source as financial weapon

"Open source always was and always will be a financial weapon by design. You see, one of the primary goals of open source was by releasing something as free, it removes the ability to make money from it."

Huntley draws parallels to historical examples:
- Linux vs. Microsoft Windows in the 1990s server market
- OpenOffice vs. Microsoft Office
- The "Halloween papers" of 1998 documenting Microsoft's internal strategy against open source

"Linux was created to destroy Microsoft's ability to make money from Microsoft Windows."

## Nation-state level implications

Unlike previous open-source campaigns that targeted individual companies, Huntley suggests we may be witnessing the first application of this strategy at the national level:

"Perhaps what we're seeing here is all-out economic warfare between one nation-state and another through the weaponisation of open source because open source was, is, and always will be a financial weapon."

The economic dynamics:
- U.S. investing "literally trillions of dollars" in frontier lab research
- Open-source models available free or for minimal cost (~$5 USD/month)
- Quality gap between frontier and open models narrowing to 2-4 months

## Strategic questions

Huntley raises critical questions about the long-term implications:

"What happens to a country that lacks AI capabilities? When their businesses depend upon AI, and thus the country's economy depends upon AI, what happens if the spigot ever gets turned off through sanctions or war?"

The dependency risk extends beyond individual businesses to entire national economies becoming reliant on AI capabilities controlled by other nations.

## Trust and business automation implications

"As we enter this weird new space where businesses are being automated with AI, it essentially hands over your business's operations to another entity."

The trust question applies equally to:
- Chinese open-source models
- Western frontier labs
- Any external provider when businesses become AI-dependent

## Strategic recommendations

For individuals and businesses, Huntley suggests:
- Students and budget-constrained users should leverage open-source models
- Businesses should build with assumption that local inferencing will become viable
- Prepare for "truly open-source models that are end-to-end reproducible"
- Consider the meta-question of trust regardless of model provider

## Future considerations

Huntley anticipates a future with:
- Local models with full training data transparency
- End-to-end reproducible model development
- Reduced dependency on any single nation's AI capabilities
- Greater awareness of the geopolitical implications of AI infrastructure

The analysis serves as both a warning about potential economic vulnerabilities and a framework for understanding the strategic dimensions of AI development and deployment.
