# Learn agents like a musical instrument

Geoff Huntley reframes how to develop competence with coding agents: an agent is
"more like a musical instrument than just a tool." A tool you read the manual for
and use deterministically; an instrument you have to *play* to get anything out
of — and skill comes from accumulated hands-on intuition, not from instructions.

## Why the reframe matters

The tool framing implies a fixed, knowable interface: learn the buttons, get the
result. But LLMs are non-deterministic, so the same prompt does not reliably
produce the same outcome. Treating the agent as an instrument fixes the right
expectation up front — you build intuition for *where it is good and where it
fails* by playing, not by memorizing rules.

## The practice

- **Play with it.** Hands-on experimentation, not documentation study, is the
  path to competence. Make discoveries through use.
- **Build intuition for the failure surface.** The goal of play is a learned
  sense of where the model is strong and where it breaks down — knowledge that
  only accrues through repeated firsthand contact.
- **This is the curiosity moat.** Huntley pairs it with the warning that the
  curious keep their jobs and those who have not been curious in two years are
  replaceable. Instrument-style play is the concrete habit behind that curiosity:
  it is how you stop being a passive consumer of the tool and develop the
  intuition that separates an operator from a button-pusher.
