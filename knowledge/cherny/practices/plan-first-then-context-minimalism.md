# Plan first — then the model outgrew it

This concept carries a genuine *arc*, not a static tip: the site captures Cherny
both advocating plan mode hard and, a year later, dropping it. Synthesis must
hold both halves, because the reversal is itself the lesson — **the era you're in
tracks the model you're using.**

**The original discipline (most of 2026).** Start most sessions in Plan mode
(shift+tab twice). Iterate on the plan with Claude until it's solid, *then* switch
to auto-accept. "A good plan is really important to avoid issues down the line."
The aim is to pour your energy into the plan so Claude can **1-shot the
implementation** — and when things go sideways, the move is not to keep pushing
but to *switch back to plan mode and re-plan*. He also tells Claude to enter plan
mode for **verification steps**, not just the build.

**The reversal (one year after GA).** For over a year plan mode was Boris's
go-to for synchronous coding; he then moved on — and "the reason is the model,
not the feature." The framing shifts from *context engineering* to **context
minimalism**: a strong enough model doesn't need an elaborate front-loaded plan
to stay on track, so the overhead of formal planning stops paying for itself. The
plan-then-1-shot habit was a workaround for a weaker model's tendency to drift.

The transferable principle: **don't treat any workflow ritual as permanent.** A
habit that was load-bearing for one model generation can become dead weight the
next. The constant underneath both eras is not "always plan" but "front-load
clarity until the model no longer needs you to" — which is exactly the
delegation-and-brief idea in [[delegate-dont-pair-program]]. Plan mode was *how*
you front-loaded clarity when the model was weaker; a sharp upfront brief is how
you do it now.
