# Nobody Has Cracked Agentic Coding

After a year of daily agentic coding, Zechner's honest summary is blunt: nobody yet knows how to do this properly. Everyone is throwing things at the wall, declaring victory, and quietly accruing tech debt. So anyone claiming to have found "the solution" should be distrusted on sight — the loudest confidence usually hides the least inspectable work.

A few principles are beginning to crystallize that yield somewhat more predictable outcomes, but outcomes remain far from reliable. Crucially, most claims of productivity gains are vibes, not measurement: there is no clean before/after, no controlled comparison, just the satisfying feeling of a churning agent. Treat that feeling as evidence of activity, not of value delivered.

The practical stance that follows is humility: hold all workflow advice loosely — including your own. Stay grounded against the hype cycle, expect today's "best practice" to look naive in six months, and don't let a confident blog post (this one included) harden into dogma.

This skepticism is the backbone of his other positions. It's why he prefers [[minimal-harness-by-subtraction]] — fewer moving parts means fewer hidden ways to be wrong — and why he refuses to over-trust agent output, leaning on [[manual-testing-only-trustworthy-oracle]] and staying [[stay-in-the-loop-not-agent-armies|in the loop]] rather than betting on autonomy he can't audit. The unifying move is to optimize for what you can verify, not for what feels fast.
