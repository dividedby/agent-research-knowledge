# Friction Builds Understanding And Taste

Zechner argues for deliberately slowing down and writing the system-defining parts by hand — or pair-programming and tab-completing through them — rather than handing them to an agent. The friction this introduces is the point, not waste. Building something up step by step is how you actually understand what you're constructing and how it should feel; the resistance is where comprehension forms.

This is also where human experience and taste live, and current models cannot replace either. Taste is the accumulated sense of what's right that you can only earn by having struggled through similar decisions. The friction is simultaneously how you keep learning and growing — outsource it and you stop developing the very judgment that makes you valuable.

The payoff is concrete. A codebase that stays maintainable because a human shaped its load-bearing structure. Fewer features, but the right ones — learning to say no is itself a feature, a discipline agents won't enforce for you. And retained agency: because you understand the design, you can go fix or refactor when it proves suboptimal, instead of being stranded in code you never really owned. Discipline and agency are non-optional, and both require humans in the work.

This pairs with [[agents-are-merchants-of-complexity]] (do the architecture by hand, since that's what compounds when delegated badly) and [[vibe-code-then-refactor-by-hand]] (let agents draft, then reclaim the design yourself). It's the constructive counterpart to [[stay-in-the-loop-not-agent-armies]]: staying in the loop isn't just defensive review, it's how you keep building taste.
