# The three prep activities AI is built for

Before any code ships, Matt frames software work as having three **prep**
activities — and argues all three are exactly where AI is "really fucking
helpful", which in hindsight should have been obvious:

- **Discuss** — figure out *what* to build. (He counts ADR-writing under this:
  deciding a hard-to-reverse trade-off is still discussion.)
- **Research** — summarize world knowledge to feed the discussion.
- **Prototype** — build something throwaway to feed the discussion.

The unifying point is that all three exist to *aid the decision*, not to be the
shipped artefact. That boundary is load-bearing: setting up infrastructure to do
work you've already decided on is **"ship", not "prep"** — you've figured out
*what*, and now you're executing. Prep is the upstream, decision-shaping phase;
prototypes and research are disposable inputs to it. The same lens recurs outside
software — most non-software activity, broken down, still reduces to discuss /
research / prototype.

## The one thing prep can't solve

The framing has a sharp limit Matt is explicit about: prep helps you decide *how*
and *whether*, but **"you can't solve the 'what to build' problem any more than an
artist can solve the 'what to paint' problem."** Discussion, research, and
prototyping all orbit a generative choice that stays human — AI accelerates the
deliberation, it doesn't originate the intent. This is the same instinct as
treating the agent as an amnesiac engineer you must not delegate your thinking to:
the agent is enormously useful across the prep activities precisely because they
*surround* the decision rather than make it.
