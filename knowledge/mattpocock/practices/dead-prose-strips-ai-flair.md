# "Dead prose": strip flair to fix AI writing output

Matt's go-to lever for bad AI writing isn't a longer style guide — it's a single
loaded phrase: **"dead prose."** Telling a model to write in dead prose means
"speak without flair, i.e. write prose without any life in it," and removing
that flair is what turns a model's writing from a decent-sounding essay into "a
decent, straightforward technical writer." The instruction targets *tone*
specifically, not content or structure — it's a compact way to ask for the
absence of the thing LLMs default to adding (rhetorical flourish, enthusiasm,
rhythm) rather than a positive spec of what good technical prose looks like.

The term isn't a recognized industry label he's borrowing — asked if it's a
known technique, he's explicit: "It's not a thing, it's just a set of words I
picked." That it works anyway is the point: the phrase is a prompt-engineering
trick, not a documented methodology, and its value is that it's short enough to
drop into a prompt on the spot rather than a rule that needs unpacking.
