# Force clarity by demanding controlled English

When a model's explanation is jargon-heavy or hand-wavy, don't ask it to
"explain more" — ask it to restate the answer in ASD-STE100 Simplified
Technical English (STE), the controlled-vocabulary, short-sentence,
one-thought-per-sentence standard aviation maintenance manuals use so a
non-native reader can't misread a safety instruction. Ball's go-to prompt,
used "in anger" repeatedly: *"what the hell do you mean? Use ASD-STE100
Simplified Technical English (STE)"* — with the STE reference copied straight
from its Wikipedia page each time.

The reason it works as a clarity forcing-function: STE's restricted vocabulary
and simple-sentence rules don't tolerate hedging or jargon-as-a-substitute-for-
understanding — a vague or confused answer stays visibly vague once it's
stripped down to short, plain sentences, where dressed-up prose can hide the
same vagueness. It's a cheap, repeatable check on whether an explanation
actually says something.
