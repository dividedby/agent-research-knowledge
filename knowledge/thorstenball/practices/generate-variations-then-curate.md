# Generate variations, then curate — don't hand-edit the fiddly stuff

For the one category of work Ball still finds himself doing by feel — the
fiddly, exploratory margin-and-word-order tweaking where you want to look,
change something, look again, undo, redo — the practice isn't to open an
editor and iterate by hand anymore. Instead: dictation-dump the raw material —
spoken ideas, screenshots, assets, and rough notes — into the agent and ask for
several distinct variations at once (he cites "15 different variations of the
widget"), then tweak and pick from what comes back.

Why this beats hand-editing: this is exactly the kind of unspecified,
feel-your-way-forward work where you don't know what you want until you see
it — the same "building is learning" gap that motivates his other practices.
Generating many options up front replaces a slow serial edit-look-edit cycle
with a single wide fan-out you can eyeball all at once and reject, tweak, or
accept, then repeat the fan-out on what you picked.

The role this puts you in is a curator, not an executor: "you feel like you're
the head chef strolling through the kitchen, spoon in hand, tasting the soup
over here, tasting the dessert over there, saying 'nah' or 'mmmmh, good' or
'into the trash.'" The agents doing the work "don't mind at all and just do
what you say and try again" — so the cost of generating and discarding a bad
variation is close to zero, which is what makes fan-out-then-taste cheaper than
iterate-by-hand once code is cheap.
